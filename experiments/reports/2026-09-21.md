### The baseline's trade count collapsed 5.5x and no config changed

The same `experiments/baseline-config.json` made **88 trades** on the 2026-09-20 window and **16**
today. Over the same two windows the 500,000 JPY baseline was essentially unchanged (803 → 812).

The cause is the lot floor, which is `min_order_size * price / total_value` and therefore scales
with the BTC price. Measured from the DB: BTC went from **¥12,231,346** at window open to
**¥13,520,000** at window close, lifting the floor from **24.46% to 27.04% of a 50,000 JPY
portfolio**. The strategy's |delta| distribution evidently has a lot of mass in that band, so a 10%
rally silently removed most of the account's ability to act.

This matters well beyond one baseline number. `MIN_TRADE_COUNT_RATIO` — the criterion that has been
the binding cause of nearly every recent rejection — is computed against that baseline. **On a
50,000 JPY account the promotion rule's trade-count criterion is substantially a measurement of
where BTC is trading, not of the hypothesis.** It is the most likely explanation for promotions
that fail to reproduce days later, and it is an argument for widening the holdout window rather
than for adjusting the ratio. Documented in PR [#31](https://github.com/SeitaHigashi/shirube/pull/31).

### `contrarian-composite-inversion` — the most informative result of the run, and it is a negative

This hypothesis tested whether shirube's technical composite carries a **negative** edge. The
motivation was shirube's own monotone dose-response: amplifying the composite is severely harmful
(`ta-weight-085-signal-amplitude`, Sharpe −14.03), damping it helps monotonically (the whole
zone-band family), and the neutral point still loses to its benchmark. Extrapolating that line past
gain 0 had never been tested. The change inverted the aggregated composite once, after the weighted
mean — flipping 100% of the TA contribution, where the earlier `contrarian-trend-subsignals`
(rejected 2026-09-15) flipped only the trend family, 0.75 of 2.75 total weight.

The agent's instrumentation confirms the change did exactly what it claimed, bit-exactly:
`max |post − (1 − pre)| = 0.0` over all 15,865 evaluated bars, with the realised band moving from
[0.2679, 0.7389] to its exact mirror [0.2611, 0.7321] and identical standard deviation.

**And the result barely moved.** At the diagnostic capitals, where the lot floor is not binding:

| Capital | Baseline return | Inverted return | Baseline trades | Inverted trades |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 500,000 | −10.8368 | −10.4301 | 812 | 791 |
| 5,000,000 | −7.3336 | −6.9988 | 818 | 792 |

Flipping the sign of the entire technical signal changed the 500,000 JPY return by **0.41 pp**, on a
run whose `fee_drag_pct` is 5.85. A signal with a real negative edge would have produced a large and
roughly symmetric swing. **Near-invariance under a total sign flip is the signature of a composite
carrying approximately zero directional information**, with the loss coming from cost and exposure
drag rather than from being systematically wrong-sided.

That re-reads the evidence that motivated the hypothesis. The monotone damping dose-response is
better explained as *damping cuts turnover and therefore cost* than as *damping approaches a sign
optimum* — which predicts that extrapolating past gain 0 buys nothing, and this run is that
prediction confirmed. **This weakens the motivation for the entire "gain axis" family of
hypotheses**, not just this variant, and it is the clearest answer the pipeline has produced to the
question "is there any gross alpha to protect?" The answer looks like no, and not because the sign
is wrong.

One reasoning error in the hypothesis is worth recording so it is not repeated. It asserted that
mirroring the target about 0.5 preserves turnover by construction. That held at the diagnostic
capitals (−2.6% and −3.2% trades) but failed badly at the primary: 16 → **79** trades. The floor
gates on `|target − current_allocation|`, and `current_allocation` is the *realised* path, which is
not the mirror of the baseline's realised path — only the target mirrors.

### The `signal_block_secs` dose curve: a throttle that is anti-throttling at the primary capital

The two parameter hypotheses filled in the interior of a curve that had data only at its ends
(0, 3600, 21600). The result was not the one either hypothesis predicted:

| Capital | Metric | baseline (0) | 900 | 1800 | 3600 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50,000 | trades | 16 | **68** | **21** | 51 |
| 50,000 | fees JPY | 312 | **1,225** | **418** | 929 |
| 50,000 | Sharpe | 6.9887 | 1.7460 | 5.5131 | 4.2567 |
| 500,000 | trades | 812 | 376 | **285** | 158 |
| 500,000 | fees JPY | 29,861 | 20,255 | **17,785** | 11,982 |
| 500,000 | Sharpe | −18.1371 | −8.8270 | **−5.6160** | −1.2228 |
| 5,000,000 | Sharpe | −11.9984 | −4.7442 | **−2.0230** | — |

(The 3600 column is the coordinator's diagnostic re-measurement of `signal-block-3600-parameter`,
already in the registry from 2026-09-20, included here to complete the curve.)

At the diagnostic capitals blocking throttles as designed and improves Sharpe enormously. **At the
primary capital it does the opposite** — 900s more than quadrupled both trades and fees. The
mechanism is the lot floor, and it is not a defect: blocking spaces how often the *signal* is
resampled, not how often the engine may act, so drift-correcting trades against a held target still
fire every bar. Freezing the target while price moves lets `target − current_alloc` accumulate
across the block instead of being chased away bar by bar, so deltas the floor would have refused
individually grow large enough to clear it. **The floor converts refusals into orders.** One agent
audited `block_start`, the live path and the simulator path against each other before trusting the
number; another independently reached the same explanation.

So the doc's standing warning that the floor can make a parameter *inert* at 50,000 JPY understates
the hazard: it can **reverse a parameter's sign**. That is now documented in PR #31.

The practical reading: `signal_block_secs: 1800` is the strongest cost-reduction result the
diagnostic capitals have produced — 65% fewer trades, 40% lower fees, Sharpe −18.14 → −5.62 at
500,000 JPY — and it is rejected because promotion is decided at 50,000 JPY, where its sign is
inverted. If the operator's account reaches the stated 500,000 JPY near-term target, this parameter
is expected to flip to strongly positive. Filing it under "another turnover hypothesis died" would
be the wrong reading.

  and promotes against. Not fixed because reconciling them is a judgement call about which is

## Leads for the next run

1. **Widen the holdout window.** Unchanged from 2026-09-20's lead #1 and strengthened materially.
   The DB now spans **43 days**, so a 21- or 28-day holdout is available today. Today added a
   mechanism for the instability, not just another instance of it: the trade-count criterion is
   partly a BTC-price measurement at 50,000 JPY (floor 24.46% → 27.04% within this single window;
   baseline 88 → 16 trades across two windows with no config change). A wider window averages over
   more of that. This is a doc change plus a routine-prompt change, not a code change, and it is
   worth more than any individual hypothesis.
2. **Reconsider whether 50,000 JPY should decide promotions.** This is the sharpest question this
   run raises and it needs a human. At the promotion capital 99.75% of intended trades are refused,
   the trade count swings 5.5x on price alone, and a throttle's sign inverts. The doc's own
   justification — "that is the operator's current account size" — is sound, but what is being
   measured there is mostly the exchange's lot size. Options a human might weigh: decide at
   500,000 JPY (the stated near-term target, where mechanisms behave as intended) and report 50,000
   as the diagnostic; or require a variant to clear the rule at *both*; or keep the rule and accept
   that promotions are statements about a heavily-suppressed regime.
3. **There is probably no gross alpha, and now for a stronger reason than "it keeps losing".**
   Inverting 100% of the technical composite moved the 500,000 JPY return by 0.41 pp on a 5.85 pp
   fee drag. The composite appears to carry approximately zero directional information — it is not
   wrong-sided, it is uninformative. If that holds up, **the entire "adjust the signal's gain, sign
   or weighting" family is a dead end**, and the productive directions are either a genuinely
   different signal (a new indicator or data source, not a reweighting of the existing five
   sub-signals) or accepting the allocation model as a cost-minimisation problem. Confirming or
   refuting this cheaply on a second window is the highest-value follow-up available.
4. **`signal_block_secs: 1800` is a strong result waiting for the right capital.** 65% fewer trades,
   40% lower fees and Sharpe −18.14 → −5.62 at 500,000 JPY. It is rejected only because its sign
   inverts at the primary capital. If lead #2 moves the promotion capital, this is the first thing
   to re-run.
5. **Blocking edges out spacing again, on a second window, at the identical 6.1% fee margin.**
   Combining them is better than either alone at 500,000 JPY but mostly overlaps. A reviewer holding
   PR #28 should weigh that it adds little on top of the already-merged #26.
6. **Two measurement fixes are still unmerged** (#29 warmup-by-bar-count, #30 reject-unknown-config-
   fields). Every number in this report is warmup-contaminated in absolute terms because #29 has not
   landed. They are cheap to review and they gate the trustworthiness of everything above.
7. **`sentiment_weight` remains untestable by construction** in this harness (2026-09-10); lead #4
   from 2026-09-14 (de-duplicating sub-signal votes) stays closed (2026-09-15).
