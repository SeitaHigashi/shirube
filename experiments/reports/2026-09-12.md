### The lot floor decided two of three verdicts

This is the run's actual finding, and it is the third consecutive run pointing at the same
place.

At 50,000 JPY with BTC near 12.2M JPY, the 0.001 BTC minimum lot is ~24.4% of the
portfolio, against an `allocation_threshold` of 0.10. The baseline wanted 6,030 rebalances
and the floor discarded 5,960 of them (98.8%). What that does to each hypothesis:

- **`reversal-hysteresis-multiplier` is untestable at the primary capital, not disproven.**
  Its 50,000 JPY report is identical to the baseline in *every field except*
  `orders_below_min` (5,960 → 1,340). The gate fired — it suppressed 4,620 would-be
  reversals — but every one of those was already being dropped by the lot floor, and the 70
  orders large enough to survive the floor were all large enough to clear `0.1 × 2.0 = 0.2`
  as well. `sharpe_absolute_delta` is exactly **0.000**. This has the same shape as the
  CoinGecko constant-volume VWMA recorded in `tried.json`, where 0.0 was arithmetic rather
  than evidence, and the doc's "Evaluation capital" section names it explicitly. Recording
  it as "rejected, no effect" would be a verdict the data cannot support. At 500,000 JPY
  the same mechanism cut trades **754 → 288 (−62%)**, doubled win rate **0.117 → 0.246**,
  and improved return **−16.09% → −11.20%** and Sharpe **−29.57 → −19.36**, with the
  buy/sell split staying near-symmetric (146/142) — i.e. it thinned the ping-pong uniformly
  rather than converting it into a directional stance.
- **`boundary-rebalance-no-trade-band` was rejected *only* on trade count.**
  `compare-backtest` scored two Pros — Sharpe +4.782 absolute, max drawdown 6.24% → 4.06% —
  and one Con: "trade count collapsed to 3% of baseline (2 vs 70) — sample too small to
  trust". The agent's own reading is the honest one and should be believed over the
  headline: at 50k the variant makes two minimum-lot buys and then never trades again, so
  `excess_return_vs_static_mix_pct` moves **−2.98 → +0.02** and `sharpe_minus_static_mix`
  **−4.91 → −0.12**. The variant did not find edge; it *became* its exposure-matched static
  mix. The promotion rule's trade-count criterion caught this correctly.
- **`rebalance-spacing-15m` had its sign flipped by the floor.** At 500k/5M, spacing
  halved the trade count and cut turnover ~36% and fees ~27%, worth +4.11pp of return and
  +8.24 Sharpe at 500k. At 50k it did the opposite: the lot floor was the *real* turnover
  brake, and widening the spacing lets the delta accumulate between checks so a larger
  fraction of evaluations produce a fillable order — `orders_below_min` collapses
  **5,960 → 567 (−90.5%)**, executed trades rise **70 → 108 (+54%)**, and turnover and fees
  rise **58%** and **50%**. Return and drawdown worsen accordingly.

The pattern across all three: **at the promotion capital the exchange's lot size, not the
config, is the strategy's dominant control parameter.** Any turnover-affecting hypothesis —
the class the doc itself nominates as highest-value — is being scored on its interaction
with the lot floor rather than on its own merit. This remains a human decision (raise the
promotion capital, or make it adaptive to the lot floor) and nothing was changed
unilaterally.

### A useful side effect

`orders_below_min` (PR #17, merged 2026-09-11) is now a working proxy for gate activity at
the primary capital *even when `total_trades` cannot move*. It is what distinguished
"the mechanism did nothing" from "the mechanism fired 4,620 times behind a floor" for
`reversal-hysteresis-multiplier`. Without it this run would have filed a false rejection.

## Enabling changes

- **#19 — two runs have chased a fix that cannot work.** `shirube backtest-data push` has
  failed with `403 Forbidden` on `DELETE /releases/assets/<id>` since 2026-09-11, and that
  report diagnosed it as a credential *scope* limitation, making "grant the routine's token
  release-asset write access" its lead #2. **That diagnosis is wrong.** The token reports
  `"permissions": {"admin": true, "maintain": true, "push": true}` on the repo, and the 403
  body (which the Rust client never surfaced — only the status) reads: *"Creating, editing,
  or deleting releases is not permitted for this session type."* It is a **Claude Code
  session-type policy applied above the token**, so no repo admin can grant it, and since
  it blocks *create* as well as delete, uploading under a fresh asset name is not a
  workaround either. Consequence: the stored DB is frozen at the 2026-09-09 snapshot and
  every run re-backfills the gap and discards it, capping the lookback at bitFlyer's 31-day
  wall permanently. The PR records two remedies, both human decisions: a human running
  `push` from a local session, or moving the store to a force-pushed orphan git branch
  (`git push` is *not* blocked for this session type). Option 2 was deliberately **not**
  implemented — it relocates the pipeline's data and writes a new branch to the repo, which
  is a human's call, not a routine's side effect. **No past report's numbers are
  invalidated**; only the attribution of the failure was wrong.

- **#20 — a measurement four separate agents have now hand-built.** `BacktestReport` had no
  buy/sell split, so the turnover *mechanism* was invisible. The 2026-09-11 ping-pong
  finding (3,744 buys vs 4,751 sells) existed only because an agent hand-instrumented the
  simulator, and **both** of today's turnover agents did the same thing independently.
  `buy_trades`/`sell_trades` are `#[serde(default)]` (so older report JSON still parses in
  `compare-backtest`) and derived so `buy + sell == total_trades` holds by construction.
  Verified decision-neutral: the unmodified baseline config reproduced **every** pre-existing
  field exactly at all three capitals. With it, today's baseline reads 379 buys / 375 sells
  — a 1.01 ratio over 14 days, the signature of oscillation rather than directional trading,
  now legible straight off the report.

- **#21 — a live, persisted knob was inert in every backtest, and fixing it exposed a
  live/backtest semantic split.** `Simulator::run` ignored `TradingConfig::rebalance_interval_secs`
  entirely, evaluating the delta on every candle at any value. The 2026-09-10 and
  2026-09-11 reports both recorded this and carried it forward *"so nobody proposes it"* —
  which left a DB-persisted, `PUT /api/config`-exposed knob permanently untestable. The fix
  is byte-identical at the default 60s (verified with `cmp` at all three capitals) and adds
  two regression tests. **But it encodes one of two defensible interpretations**, and the PR
  says so prominently: the live `TradingEngine` does *not* use the field as a throttle —
  `handle_indicator` evaluates the delta and may order on **every** `IndicatorOutput`,
  ungated, and `SignalEngine::run` broadcasts those on a **250 ms** ticker, so live the
  delta is evaluated ~4×/second and the field only paces a secondary drift check. A human
  must decide whether the engine or the simulator is wrong before anyone tunes this field.

### Suspected defects found and deliberately not fixed

- **The live engine evaluates the full allocation delta at ~4 Hz.** `SignalEngine::run`
  republishes `IndicatorOutput` every 250 ms off cached indicator values, and
  `handle_indicator`'s order path is ungated by `rebalance_interval_secs`. This is plausibly
  a far larger source of over-trading than any config knob this pipeline has tested, and it
  means the backtest (one evaluation per 60s candle) and the engine differ by ~240× in
  evaluation frequency. **Not fixed**: it is on the live order-submission path and the
  hypothesis that surfaced it explicitly forbade touching live scheduling. It deserves its
  own hypothesis — gate `handle_indicator`'s order path on the indicator point's `time`
  actually advancing — which would also make #21's semantic question answer itself.
- **`Simulator::run` writes each run into the shared DB** via `backtest_runs().insert()`, so
  the "read-only" `./run.db` that every parallel worktree agent points at is in fact written
  concurrently. It distorted nothing this run (the insert happens after the report is
  computed, and bars are only read), but it is a latent write-contention hazard across
  parallel agents. Left alone as out of scope for a variant branch.
- **`MockExchangeClient::volume_jpy` is a lifetime accumulator, never windowed to a trailing
  30 days.** Carried unchanged from 2026-09-11; still a human call, still on the live
  paper-trading path.
- **`--warmup-candles` under-delivers on a sparse tape** (see "Warmup caveat"). Requested
  300, got 177, below `sma_period: 200`. The CLI reports the shortfall honestly and the
  effect is 0.15% of evaluated candles, so this is recorded rather than fixed.

## New hypotheses generated this run

All 25 hypothesis files on disk were already in `experiments/tried.json` (`CANDIDATES` was
**empty**), so step 3 generated the maximum of 3, and all three were tried the same run.

- **`boundary-rebalance-no-trade-band`** (algorithm, literature-motivated): rebalance to the
  near edge of the no-trade band rather than all the way to target. Motivated by Ma & Smith,
  arXiv:2411.07949 (see "Literature reviewed"), whose claim is that a band with hysteresis
  minimises trading frequency at fixed return, and by the observation that shirube has the
  band's entry half but snaps to the centre on every trigger — so an over-correction
  immediately manufactures the opposite delta. Pros/Cons carried into the file's
  `paper_reference`.
- **`reversal-hysteresis-multiplier`** (algorithm): an opposite-signed delta must clear
  `allocation_threshold × 2.0` while a continuation clears the unchanged threshold. This
  implements verbatim the recommendation the 2026-09-11 report ended on and which had never
  been tested — *"add hysteresis/cooldown so a lot-sized overshoot cannot trigger its own
  reversal"* — grounded in this run's baseline showing an 11.7% win rate at 500k, i.e. seven
  trades in eight losing, which is what a position oscillating across one threshold looks
  like.
- **`rebalance-spacing-15m`** (algorithm): `rebalance_interval_secs` 60 → 900, carrying its
  prerequisite enabling fix on a separate branch. Proposed specifically to *close* the
  standing lead that the previous two reports carried forward "so nobody proposes it" —
  a live, persisted knob being inert in the backtest is a measurement defect to fix, not a
  reason to leave the knob untested.

**Leads for the next generation cycle**, in rough order of promise:

1. **The 50,000 JPY promotion capital is still not measuring hypotheses.** Third consecutive
   run. Last run it was degenerate (1 trade); this run it trades but the lot floor decided
   two of three verdicts and flipped the sign of the third. A human decision, and it
   supersedes everything below.
2. **Gate the live engine's order path on the indicator time advancing** (the 4 Hz finding
   above). Larger than any knob tested so far, and it would resolve #21's open question.
3. **Re-run `reversal-hysteresis-multiplier` at a capital where the lot floor does not
   bind.** Its 500k result (−62% trades, win rate doubled, +4.89pp return) is the strongest
   mechanism result this pipeline has produced; it simply could not be scored where it was
   scored. A fresh `content_hash` is not needed if the promotion capital changes — the
   registry entry records it as untestable rather than refuted.
4. **A hypothesis that raises `allocation_threshold` above the lot fraction (~0.245) at
   50k**, making the real gate explicit rather than silent. Note that a hypothesis which
   only moves the threshold *below* ~0.245 is provably inert at the primary capital, since
   an order under the lot is dropped exactly when `|delta| <` the lot fraction — the two
   gates are algebraically identical.
5. **`rebalance_interval_secs` is no longer untestable** (PR #21), but must not be tuned
   until the live/backtest semantics are reconciled.
6. **The volatility-scaling lead stays closed**, per 2026-09-10.
