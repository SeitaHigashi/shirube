### `h1-block-held-signal` — promoted, and what it does and does not show

Holding the composite constant inside UTC-aligned 1-hour blocks cut the number of distinct composite
values from 15,393 (one per candle) to **336** — a 45.8× reduction, against yesterday's 271× at H6 —
and improved every strategy metric at every capital: Sharpe −11.696 → **−7.624** at 50k,
−31.088 → **−14.547** at 500k, −24.671 → **−12.114** at 5M, with drawdown down 2.56pp / 8.29pp /
6.25pp. Unlike yesterday's three variants it kept enough turnover to be scoreable: 40 trades against
the 38-trade floor. The sign is the same at all three capitals, so there is no divergence to weigh.

Three things keep this honest:

- **`sharpe_minus_static_mix` improves from −6.814 to −2.741 but stays negative.** As with H6, a
  slower clock moves the result **toward** the exposure-matched static mix from below without
  crossing it. This is not evidence of gross alpha, and the standing description of the system is
  unchanged.
- **The band barely narrows** (sd 0.0739 → 0.0725, −2.0%; range −6.4%), which is what separates this
  from the band-width family: blocking removes intra-hour jitter, not amplitude. So the two
  mechanisms that have now both worked really are attacking different axes.
- **The mechanism did not demonstrate the separation its rationale claimed.** Of 40 trades at 50k,
  **39 fire on the first candle of a block and exactly 1 is a genuine mid-block drift correction**
  (165/1 at the larger capitals). The drift path is structurally live and did fire, but with
  `allocation_threshold: 0.1` price must move the allocation 10 percentage points inside one hour to
  trigger it, which essentially never happens. In *realised* behaviour this variant is therefore
  nearly indistinguishable from an hourly action throttle — even though `rebalance-spacing-15m`
  (rejected) was a different mechanism, the observable difference between them in this window is one
  trade. The agent flagged this itself rather than letting the rationale stand, and it is flagged in
  the PR.

### `flat-zone-slope-damping` — the band-width curve now has a bracket

The zone `0.0/1.0` was verified **empirically, not assumed**, to be exactly an ×0.8 compression of
the baseline's allocation target about neutral: over the 15,393 evaluated candles
`max |variant_target − (0.5 + 0.8·(baseline_target − 0.5))| = 5.55e-17`, and the two runs' raw
composite traces are element-wise identical (the control that makes the comparison clean). Neither
zone's saturation arm was hit even once — realised raw band [0.336286, 0.654519]. So this is the
damping mechanism with **no code, no new config field, and no live-path risk**, and if a usable dose
exists it ships as a two-number config edit.

It was rejected on trade count alone (10 vs the 38 floor) while passing Sharpe by +6.247 — **the
largest Sharpe gain this pipeline has ever measured at the promotion capital** — and drawdown by
3.46pp. Combined with yesterday's 0.75 result this **brackets the collapse**: damping 1.00 → 76
trades, 0.80 → 10 trades, 0.75 → 1 trade. Any promotable damping dose lies well above 0.8, and the
collapse between 1.0 and 0.8 is far steeper than the Sharpe gain across the same interval. As with
every turnover cut, `sharpe_minus_static_mix` improved sharply (−6.814 → −0.567) without crossing
zero.

Cost effect, scored on `total_fees_jpy` as the doc requires: fees fell 84.4% at 50k (1,292 → 202),
27.5% at 500k and 27.2% at 5M, while `effective_fee_pct` **rose** at all three (0.1282% → 0.1482%,
0.0652% → 0.0738%, 0.0260% → 0.0293%) — the doc's sublinearity caveat confirmed again, and standing
as always on `MockExchangeClient`'s from-zero, never-expiring volume accumulator.

### `conviction-gated-rebalance` — rejected, and it falsified its own premise

This is the run's most instructive failure. The gate suppressed **7,513 of 15,393** allocation-delta
evaluations (48.8%), and above the lot floor it did exactly what it was designed to do: at 500k and
5M, volume fell 38% and fees fell ~29%, with Sharpe improving −31.09 → −21.88 and −24.67 → −17.45.

At the **promotion capital the lot floor inverted it**. Suppressing half the evaluations let the
allocation gap widen between the evaluations that survived, so *more* of the surviving orders cleared
the 0.001 BTC floor: `orders_below_min` fell 5,877 → **2,504** while trades **rose** 76 → 97 and fees
**rose 23%** (1,292 → 1,589). The designated cost-targeting hypothesis increased cost at the capital
that decides. Sharpe worsened by 3.654 and drawdown by 0.88pp; only trade count passed.

The rationale's central quantitative premise was also measured and found wrong. It argued the 50k lot
floor needs ~0.245 allocation units of gap ≈ a composite excursion of ~0.196, far above the 0.06
gate, so the gate would pass over the primary capital untouched. The measured composite conviction
**never exceeds 0.1637** (median 0.0616, mean 0.0635) — it never reaches that excursion at all, which
is why the floor is cleared by *accumulated drift* rather than by signal strength. And 0.06 sits
almost exactly on the median, making the gate a ~50/50 split rather than the tail filter it was
designed as. Both corrections are worth carrying forward: **the lot floor at 50k is cleared by drift
accumulation, not by conviction**, and any future conviction-scaled parameter should be sized against
the measured distribution above rather than against a derived excursion.

### Suspected defects found and deliberately not fixed

- **`backtest-variant` writes to the DB it is documented as reading.** `Simulator::run` ends with
  `self.db.backtest_runs().insert(...)`, so every invocation appends a row to the shared `run.db`,
  including the three agents' runs against the file the coordinator called read-only. It touches only
  the `backtest_runs` table and every run was bit-reproducible across repeated invocations while
  sibling agents hit the same file, so nothing this run measured was distorted. Not fixed, because
  suppressing the insert changes documented behaviour for every caller. **New this run**, and worth a
  human's call: either the harness doc should stop calling the DB read-only, or `backtest-variant`
  should take a flag.
- **`sharpe_minus_static_mix` is not a like-for-like delta between rows.** The static-mix benchmark
  is rebuilt at each run's *own* `avg_btc_exposure`, which moves with the variant (0.5165 → 0.4683
  for the conviction gate at 50k). That is the documented intent of exposure matching, but it means
  candidate-minus-baseline on that metric compares against two different benchmarks. Worth stating
  wherever the metric is quoted as a delta. **New this run.**

- **`flat-zone-slope-damping`** (parameter): set the zone to `0.0/1.0`, which within the realised

  the observation that the mechanism that gained +10.19 Sharpe at 500k yesterday needs no new config
  field to express.
- **`conviction-gated-rebalance`** (algorithm, literature-motivated): gate order submission on
  `|composite − 0.5| < min_conviction` via a new field defaulting to 0.0, applied at both the live and
  backtest sites without touching `compute_btc_target`. Motivated by arXiv:2606.00060's cost-justified

  hypothesis. This is the first gate this pipeline has ever put on the signal's own strength rather
  than on the position gap or the clock.
- **`h1-block-held-signal`** (algorithm, literature-motivated): quantise the composite onto
  UTC-aligned 1-hour blocks — the same mechanism as yesterday's H6 at a 60× rather than 360× slowdown,
  dosed specifically to clear `MIN_TRADE_COUNT_RATIO`. Motivated by yesterday's finding that H6 gave
  the largest single-variant improvement ever measured and died only on trade count.

**Leads for the next generation cycle**, in rough order of promise:

1. **Fit the two dose-response curves instead of sampling them.** Both now have a bracket at the
   promotion capital. Band width: 1.00 → 76 trades, 0.80 → 10, 0.75 → 1, with Sharpe monotonically
   improving — so test **0.90 and 0.95**, where the trade count plausibly survives. Signal clock:
   60s → 76 trades, 3600s → 40 trades (promoted), 21600s → 1 trade — so test **900s and 1800s**,
   which should sit between and may beat H1. These are the two highest-value experiments available
   and both are now well-posed rather than speculative.
2. **Combine the two mechanisms, now that one of them is promotable.** This was 2026-09-16's lead #3
   and today strengthens it: band width and signal clock were measured to act on different axes (H1
   narrowed sd by only 2.0%), and H1 leaves trade-count headroom. A 1-hour block at damping ~0.9 is
   the obvious composite test.
3. **`MIN_TRADE_COUNT_RATIO` is no longer demonstrably blocking, but the promotion *capital* still
   is.** Yesterday's lead #1 asked for a human decision on the ratio; today's promotion shows the
   rule can be cleared. What today sharpens instead is the *capital*: `conviction-gated-rebalance`

   throttle but a sign-flipper. Raising the promotion capital to 500,000 JPY remains a human decision

4. **Size any future conviction-scaled parameter against the measured distribution**, not against a
