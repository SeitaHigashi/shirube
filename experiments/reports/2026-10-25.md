**Every variant cleared the Sharpe and drawdown criteria by a wide margin. All three were rejected only because they traded fewer than 50% of the baseline's 107 trades.** Read that verdict carefully:

- **The 50k variant runs are degenerate.** Each made only the opening buy (or 5 trades) and then hit the lot floor, so each is essentially a static mix:
  - `sharpe_minus_static_mix`: +0.001, +0.001 and −0.04.
  - Their "improvement" is mostly the baseline's 107 harmful trades being removed. It is not evidence that the signal is good.
- **In this window the trade-count criterion works against the promotion rule's purpose.** The baseline's turnover is what loses money (−6.64 pp vs static), so any variant that stops that turnover fails the 50%-of-baseline floor. This is the mirror image of 2026-10-22, where the baseline made 1 trade and nothing could be told apart. In neither run could the 50k gate measure signal quality.
- **The diagnostics (500k / 5M) are informative and consistent with 2026-10-19/22:**
  - `equal-weight-rebalance-15m` is the best result on record at 500k:
    - Sharpe −29.11 → −6.52, return −15.90% → −3.78%, DD 15.93% → 4.59%.
    - Trades 758 → 56, fees 28,201 → 3,841 JPY.
    - It still loses to the static mix (−1.39 pp, −2.50 Sharpe).
  - At reversion 1.0 / trend 1.0 without spacing (2026-10-22, another window) the figure was −2.80%. The spacing's own contribution cannot be separated across windows.
  - **The trend-weight curve turns.** On today's window at 500k:
    - trend 1.0 / reversion 0.5 gives −5.64%.
    - trend 1.0 / reversion 0.25 gives −9.55%.
    - Cutting the reversion family below half makes things worse again. The optimum sits near equal weights, not at the trend-dominant end.
  - No variant's direction flips across capitals: each improves on the baseline at all three. The 50k improvement is a lot-floor artifact, as noted above.

## New hypotheses generated this run

- **trend-full-reversion-half-current-config** (algorithm): literature-motivated, from Tang et al. 2024 (arXiv:2402.11930). Short-interval BTC Hurst exponent went from 0.42 to 0.49, i.e. the short-horizon reversion edge is fading. It also follows 2026-10-22 lead #2 (walk the weight curve past 1.0).
  - Pros: same asset, high-frequency, recent sample.
  - Cons: descriptive study, no costs, and near-random-walk evidence cuts against trend persistence too.
- **equal-weight-rebalance-15m-current-config** (algorithm): 2026-10-22 lead #3, combining the two best diagnostic effects. It is also the cost-side hypothesis the warning rule requires.
- **trend-weighted-subsignals-current-config** (algorithm): a re-run of `trend-weighted-subsignals` (trend 1.0 / reversion 0.25) on the current config, which removes the stale zone 0.2/0.8 confound and completes the weight curve.
