All three `sharpe_improvement_pct` values above are `compare-backtest`'s
absolute Sharpe delta, not a percentage — the baseline Sharpe is
non-positive (-41.43), so the tool reports `sharpe_improvement_pct: null`
and falls back to the absolute-delta threshold (>= 0.1) instead. The
same absolute-delta numbers are what's stored in each `tried.json` entry's
`sharpe_improvement_pct` field for this run, since the schema doesn't
have a separate field for the non-percentage case.

Verdict detail per `shirube compare-backtest`:
- **shorter-hourly-indicator-periods**: Sharpe improved by 14.81 (a real,
  large improvement) and trade count is healthy at 151% of baseline, but
  it was rejected purely on the "max drawdown must not worsen" rule —
  drawdown ticked up by 0.0020 percentage points (4.2896% → 4.2915%),
  which is almost certainly simulation noise rather than a meaningful
  regression, but the promotion rule (correctly, per its own design) does
  not carve out an epsilon for "worsened by a hair."
- **lower-allocation-threshold**: rejected outright — Sharpe got *worse*
  (-1.96 absolute) and drawdown worsened by 0.30pp. Isolating
  `allocation_threshold` alone (0.15 → 0.05) with default indicator
  periods let more low-conviction rebalances through, which hurt more
  than it helped once the underlying signal was directional and noisy
  most of the time.
- **continuous-crossover-subsignals**: touches
  `TradingEngine::compute_btc_target`'s body (confirmed, not its
  signature) per the 2026-09-08 policy allowing this on a dedicated
  worktree branch. `cargo test` passed 186/186 including the updated
  `compute_btc_target_*` expected values. Sharpe improved by 16.54 (the
  best of the three) and drawdown *improved* by 1.33pp — but trade count
  collapsed to 27% of baseline (10 vs 37), well under the 50% floor, so
  it was rejected on sample-size grounds despite otherwise looking like
  the strongest candidate. Its worktree and branch
  (`worktree-agent-a87b70de0e8f02f46`) were deleted after the rejection
  per the pipeline's cleanup step — nothing to PR.
