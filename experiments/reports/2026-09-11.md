### The 50,000 JPY cliff — this run's actual finding

The DB restored fine and the data is real, but the primary capital has fallen off
a discontinuity. Sweeping capital over the identical window:

| Capital | Trades (14d) | Return % | Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|
| **50,000 (promotion capital)** | **1** | -2.2916 | -4.1349 |
| 100,000 | 463 | -8.8988 | -15.1705 |
| 200,000 | 729 | -9.1751 | -15.5013 |
| 500,000 | 729 | -7.4570 | -12.4297 |
| 1,000,000 | 729 | -6.0712 | -9.9886 |
| 5,000,000 | 729 | -4.1173 | -6.6066 |

Between 50,000 and 100,000 JPY the trade count goes **1 → 463**. This is the
lot-size floor described under "Evaluation capital", but sharper than the doc
records it: BTC averaged **12,329,986 JPY** over this window, so the 0.001 BTC
minimum is **~24.7%** of a 50,000 JPY portfolio against an `allocation_threshold`
of 0.10. The floor binds whenever `min_lot_notional / equity > allocation_threshold`
— 24.7% > 10% at 50k, 2.5% < 10% at 500k — and it is a cliff, not a gradient.
The effect compounds: as a losing 50k account shrinks, the lot becomes a *larger*
fraction of it, suppressing still more rebalances.

PR #17's new `orders_below_min` counter quantifies it directly: over this window
the 50,000 JPY baseline **wanted 5,486 rebalances, executed 1, and discarded
5,485** before they ever reached `RiskManager::evaluate`. At 500,000 and
5,000,000 JPY the same counter reads **0**.

The doc chose 50,000 JPY as the sole promotion capital on 2026-09-11 based on the
2026-08-10..09-09 window, where it still produced 286 trades. It no longer does.
**Consequences, for a human to decide on:**

1. The promotion rule's trade-count criterion is now trivially satisfied —
   "≥ 50% of baseline's" means "≥ 0.5 trades". Both of today's rejects scored
   "Pro: sample size acceptable" at 11,200% and 310,100% of baseline.
2. The Sharpe criterion is comparing a candidate against a static hold curve, so
   it measures exposure, not timing.
3. Any turnover-affecting hypothesis — the class the doc itself nominates as
   highest-value — is untestable at the primary capital.

This is a human decision (raise the primary capital, or make the promotion
capital adaptive to the lot floor), not something to change unilaterally
mid-pipeline, so nothing was changed. It is lead #1 for the next run.

- **At 50,000 JPY the two gates are perfectly redundant**: the baseline's 5,485

  trade, but with 5,485 suppressed by the fee threshold and 0 by the lot floor.

### `min-notional-aware-rebalance` — rejected; correct diagnosis, backwards remedy

Round a sub-lot rebalance **up** to `min_order_size` when affordable, instead of
dropping it. Primary: **-53.85%** return, Sharpe **-97.27**, max DD **53.90%**,
3,101 trades, 14 circuit-breaker trips in 14 days.

The failure mechanism is fully accounted for by the agent's instrumentation
(the tallies close exactly to the reported 3,101 fills): a rounded-up order is a
**24.7% position swing on a 50,000 JPY account, taken to correct a ~10% delta**.
It overshoots, which immediately makes the *opposite* delta clear the threshold —
visible as near-symmetric 3,744 buys / 4,751 sells. The result is a min-lot
ping-pong that moved 38.4M JPY of notional on a 50,000 JPY account (768x
turnover) and paid 26,062 JPY in fees: `fee_drag_pct` **52.1%** against a
53.85% loss, i.e. **essentially the entire loss is commission**, while the
underlying only moved -4.98%.

Running the *baseline* config with rounding gives -53.72% / 3,100 trades, so the
damage is attributable to the rounding and not to that hypothesis's zone change.

**The hypothesis's diagnosis was right and its remedy was backwards.** On a small
account the exchange lot is a coarser quantum than the strategy's own tolerance
band, so rounding up is strictly worse than dropping. Any future attempt should
go the other way — require `|delta|` to exceed the lot's own notional fraction
(making the real gate explicit and *raising* the threshold), or add
hysteresis/cooldown so a lot-sized overshoot cannot trigger its own reversal.

## Enabling changes

- **#16 — the loop could not run at all in the cloud routine.** `reqwest` is built
  with the `rustls-tls` feature, whose trust anchors are the Mozilla roots baked
  into the binary; unlike an OpenSSL-backed client it does not consult
  `SSL_CERT_FILE`. Behind the routine's TLS-terminating egress proxy, *every*
  outbound call failed with `invalid peer certificate: UnknownIssuer` — both
  `backtest-data pull` and `backfill-executions`, so the data bootstrap could not
  start. `curl` against the same hosts returned 200, isolating it to shirube's own
  trust store. Fixed by a shared `src/http.rs` that keeps the built-in roots and
  *additionally* trusts the PEM bundle named by `SSL_CERT_FILE` /
  `REQUESTS_CA_BUNDLE` when set; hosts setting neither are unaffected. This is the
  same class of silent cloud-only breakage as the `gh`/`sqlite3` dependency
  removed in `c868455`. **No past report's numbers are invalidated** — the failure
  was total, so no wrong number was ever produced by it.

- **#17 — a 5,485-event effect was invisible in every report.** `orders_below_min`
  counts rebalances the strategy wanted and the lot size forbade. Because
  `allocation_delta_to_order` returns `None` *before* `RiskManager::evaluate`,
  these appeared in neither `total_trades` nor `orders_rejected`. Verified
  decision-neutral twice independently (agent, then coordinator) — every
  pre-existing field bit-for-bit identical at all three capitals. **Past reports
  are not wrong, but they are incomplete**: any report whose baseline ran at a
  capital where the floor binds understated how much the strategy wanted to trade.
  At 50,000 JPY over this window that gap is 5,485 vs 1.

- **CI on `dev` was red and blocking every PR this loop opens.** Runs 64-67 all
  failed both `nix build` and `nix flake check` with a fixed-output hash mismatch;
  run 63 (`8b3485c`) was the last green. Root cause: `ccfd3a9` added
  `flate2 = "1"` to `Cargo.toml`/`Cargo.lock` for the `backtest-data` gzip path
  without updating `flake.nix`'s `cargoHash`. `flake.lock` is pinned, so this was
  a stale vendor hash, not nixpkgs drift. I derived the corrected hash
  independently and it matched **#15** (opened 12:04 UTC by another session)
  byte-for-byte, so rather than open a duplicate I cherry-picked #15's commit onto
  #16 to get it green. **#15 is still the right place to review and merge this**;
  it no-ops on #16 once merged.

### Suspected defects found and deliberately not fixed

- **`MockExchangeClient::volume_jpy` is a lifetime accumulator, never windowed to
  a trailing 30 days** (`src/exchange/mock.rs`, read by `fee_pct()`). bitFlyer's
  schedule is trailing-30-day volume. Over a 14-day holdout the distortion is
  small, but it (a) makes the live paper-trading account's tier ratchet down and
  never decay, permanently understating fees, and (b) is what makes the
  `fee-tier-aware` lockout above irreversible — with real 30-day decay the tier
  would reset. **Not fixed deliberately**: it sits inside that hypothesis's own
  "MUST NOT change the fee logic" constraint, it is on the live paper-trading
  path, and changing it would shift every number already recorded under
  `experiments/` — that is a human call, not a side effect of a routine run.
- **`Simulator::run` does `let _ = exchange.send_order(&r).await;`** — an order the
  exchange refuses is discarded and counted nowhere, the same blind spot
  `orders_below_min` was created to close. Measured at 0 occurrences this run, so
  it distorted nothing; left alone rather than widening a rejected hypothesis's
  diff. Worth its own small PR.
- **Hypothesis files can silently bundle unrelated `trading_config` changes.**
  Both of today's hypotheses shipped a zone change (0.2/0.8) that their
  `code_change_summary` never mentions, making the raw A/B uninterpretable until
  an agent ran ablations by hand. Worth either diff-checking each hypothesis's
  `trading_config` against `experiments/baseline-config.json` and requiring every
  differing key to be justified, or making the mechanism-only ablation a standing
  part of the algorithm-agent prompt. Not fixed — it is a change to the pipeline's
  own rules and belongs in `docs/self-improvement-loop.md` with a human's
  agreement.
