Verdict detail per `shirube compare-backtest`:

- **mean-reversion-weighted-subsignals** — **promoted, and by a wide margin:
  +2.240 Sharpe, nearly triple the previous best promotion (+0.848 on
  2026-09-08).** Every metric moved the right way: return −3.86% → −2.62%,
  Sharpe −6.533 → −4.294, max drawdown 4.40% → 3.35%, win rate 8.3% → 66.7%,
  trade count 80% of baseline. It is the exact **mirror** of Run 3's rejected
  `trend-weighted-subsignals` (trend 1.0 / reversion 0.25, that run's worst
  result on every axis), which is what motivated testing the other direction,
  and it is independently supported by pre-holdout data: the lag-1
  autocorrelation of hourly BTC/JPY returns over the 76-day training window is
  **−0.028**, weakly mean-reverting at exactly the resolution the engine trades
  at. Touches `compute_btc_target`'s body; **signature verified byte-for-byte
  unchanged, and `cargo test` (203 passed) re-run by the coordinator in the
  worktree rather than taken on the agent's report.** Diff confined to
  `src/trading/engine.rs`.
  **Two cautions for the reviewer.** First, `win_rate` 0.667 alongside a −2.62%
  return means the losing third of round-trips is far larger than the winning
  two thirds — a high hit rate with negative expectancy, the classic shape of
  cutting winners short. Second, n=1: one 14-day window, on a −0.028
  autocorrelation that is weak in absolute terms. The size of the improvement
  is as much a reason for scrutiny as for confidence.
- **wide-zone-band** — promoted. The untested opposite of Run 4's rejected
  `narrow-zone-band` (0.35/0.65), which had *doubled* turnover and made every
  metric worse, yielding the conclusion that "the zone band is the binding
  constraint on turnover, and turnover is not the deficit". Widening to
  0.1/0.9 lowers the interpolation slope from 1/0.6 to 1/0.8 and, with the
  combined signal spanning only [0.15, 0.85], puts zones A and C out of reach
  entirely — every rebalance becomes an interior partial move. The mechanical
  prediction held (25 → 20 trades, 1.79 → 1.43 trades/day) and this time the
  metrics followed: Sharpe +0.491, drawdown −0.28pp. **Note the two promoted
  variants were measured independently, each against the same baseline; their
  effects have not been measured in combination.**
- **realized-vol-scaled-exposure** — rejected, and the most informative
  negative of the run. Sharpe fell 0.140 (below the ≥0.1 improvement bar in the
  wrong direction), though drawdown improved 0.43pp and trade count rose to
  128% of baseline. `cargo test` 213 passed with 11 new tests; signature
  verified unchanged.
  **The reason it failed is a regime-transfer failure, not a failure of the
  mechanism.** The agent instrumented the actual factor: over the 336 evaluated
  candles `k` deviated from 1.0 on **312 (92.9%)**, so this was a real test and
  not a null — but the *direction* was inverted. Holdout realised volatility
  (mean **0.320%/h**) came in materially **below** the hard-coded
  `REFERENCE_HOURLY_VOL = 0.0042` (0.42%/h) fitted on the training window, so
  `k` had mean **1.312** and sat pinned at the `VOL_SCALE_MAX` amplification cap
  for 61% of the window. The rule therefore acted predominantly as an **exposure
  amplifier**, which is the opposite of the volatility damper Moreira & Muir
  describe. Exactly the failure mode the hypothesis's own `paper_reference.cons`
  predicted ("the specific constants here come from shirube's own pre-holdout
  training window and from judgement, not from the paper"), and exactly what the
  doc's design intends: the paper's alphas never substituted for shirube's own
  backtest gate.

## New hypotheses generated this run

`CANDIDATES` was empty: all 14 pre-existing `experiments/hypotheses/*.json`
files hash-matched entries already in `experiments/tried.json`. **For the first
time this was pure hash matching with no name fallback** — `shirube
hypothesis-hash` (PR #8) reproduced all 14 stored hashes exactly, closing the
ambiguity that five consecutive runs had to work around. Three new hypotheses
were generated per the doc's Step 3, and all three were tried the same run, so
none carry over to tomorrow.

- **wide-zone-band** (parameter): grounded in Run 4's `narrow-zone-band`
  rejection, which established the zone band as the binding turnover control
  and showed that *more* turnover destroyed value — leaving the opposite
  direction as the obvious untested probe, and a cheap one (two numbers change).
- **mean-reversion-weighted-subsignals** (algorithm): grounded in Run 3's
  `trend-weighted-subsignals` being that run's worst result on every axis, plus
  the −0.028 lag-1 autocorrelation of hourly returns measured on the 76-day
  pre-holdout training window.
- **realized-vol-scaled-exposure** (algorithm, literature-motivated — Moreira &
  Muir 2017): grounded in Run 4's explicit lead that "a regime hypothesis needs
  a reference the composite doesn't contain (realised volatility, a
  longer-horizon trend, or drawdown state)" after `sma-regime-gate` turned out
  redundant with the composite's own SMA-cross term. Realised return volatility
  was the first item on that list.

**Leads for the next generation cycle**, in rough order of promise:

1. **The two promotions have never been measured together.** `wide-zone-band`
   and `mean-reversion-weighted-subsignals` were each scored against the same
   unmodified baseline. If a human merges both, the next baseline carries an
   untested interaction; if only one merges, the other's measured delta no
   longer applies. A combined variant is the single highest-value next test
   either way.
2. **Re-centre the volatility reference rather than abandoning it.**
   `realized-vol-scaled-exposure` failed because a *fixed* constant fitted on
   the training window did not transfer — the holdout's volatility *level*, not
   just its variation, had shifted. A trailing/rolling reference (e.g. the
   median realised vol over the preceding N candles) makes the rule
   scale-invariant and is a genuinely different hypothesis, not a re-run of
   this one.
3. **Sweep the new family weights.** 1.0 / 0.25 was chosen only as the mirror
   of a rejected variant, not because anything says 0.25 is right. 0.5 and 0.75
   are cheap probes on a curve nobody has walked, and the promoted point sits at
   one end of it.
4. **Turn on `--warmup-candles` in a run that re-baselines.** See the second
   "not fixed" item above — ~60% of every scored window currently runs without
   the two longest-horizon indicators.
